# Study Guide Format

Use this when I ask for a study guide, review sheet, cheat sheet, or practice questions.

## Ground rules

- Build from my notes, textbook chapter, or topic first. Don't invent facts my notes don't support.
- If you add something from general knowledge, say so and tell me to check it against my textbook or teacher.
- If something in my notes looks wrong or missing, flag it instead of quietly fixing it.
- Never put answers to my graded or assigned work in the guide. If I paste a worksheet, build the guide on the concepts it covers, with new example problems.
- Keep it short: usually 1 to 4 pages. Leave out filler.

## Ask only if needed

Ask at most one or two quick questions, and only if you can't tell:

- What subject, unit, or test is it for?
- Do I have notes or a chapter to build from?

If I gave enough already, just build it.

## Structure

Pick the parts that fit the subject. Not every guide needs every part.

1. **Title and subject**
2. **Sections by topic**, each with a one or two sentence summary and the key points
3. **Key terms**, with short definitions in plain words
4. **Worked example** with new numbers or a new scenario, shown step by step (math and science)
5. **Watch out for**: common mistakes
6. **Study tips**: only when they genuinely help (a pattern, a mnemonic)
7. **Practice questions** (5 to 10), mixing easy and hard
8. **Answer key** on its own page at the end, so I can cover it up

Make the guide something I can use to test myself, not just reread.

## Formatting

- Short headings, short bullet lists, plain words.
- For math and chemistry, write subscripts and exponents clearly (H2O can be written H₂O in text; in a PDF, check they display correctly).
- If I ask for a PDF and you can create files, make one. If not, give the guide as formatted text I can paste into a document, and say so.

## After you give it to me

- Give a one or two sentence summary of what's in it.
- Say if you added anything that wasn't in my notes.
- Offer to quiz me on it, or make a shorter cheat sheet or a Blooket set. Quizzing me is often more useful than rereading.
